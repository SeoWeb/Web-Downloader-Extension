A PagePocket save is a self-contained copy of the page as your browser sees
it: the HTML, the stylesheets, the images, and the documents it links. This
page explains what happens during a save, what you can capture, and where the
copy ends up.

## What happens during a save

When you click **Start download** in the side panel, the extension works
through three phases, with progress shown for each:

1. **Scraping** — the page is scrolled so lazily-loaded content appears, and
   its resources are collected.
2. **Uploading** — each resource (stylesheets, scripts, images, documents) is
   uploaded, with a running count.
3. **Assembling** — the cloud pipeline packages everything into the stored
   copy and indexes it for search.

If a few non-essential resources fail to upload, the save still completes and
the panel tells you how many items failed. Keep the panel open and stay on
the tab until the save finishes — closing it can abort the capture.

## Choosing what to capture

The panel lets you pick which parts of the page are included: the HTML,
images, linked pages (saved as HTML files), assets like CSS and JS, documents
like PDFs, or the content as plain text. For most archiving, the defaults are
right.

## Cloud saves and local exports

Where the copy goes depends on the **PagePocket Cloud Storage** switch:

- **On (default for signed-in users)** — the page is saved to your private
  cloud library. It shows up in the [app](/app) on every device you sign in
  from, becomes [searchable](/docs/full-text-search), and can be
  [shared](/docs/sharing) with a link. Cloud saves count against your plan:
  50 pages per rolling 30 days and 500 MB on Free. You can pull any cloud
  save back down as a ZIP at any time — **Download** in the page viewer or
  the ⋯ menu on a dashboard card (see [Getting started](/docs/getting-started)).
- **Off** — the same capture is handed to you directly as a download: a ZIP
  archive with the full page structure, or a single HTML file that inlines
  everything (best for quick archiving of simple pages). Local exports never
  touch your account or quota — they are ordinary files in your Downloads
  folder.

There is also a **full website** mode, which follows the first level of
linked pages and saves each one — useful when a set of pages belongs
together. It takes noticeably longer than a single page.

## Viewing an archived page

Saved pages open in a sandboxed viewer that renders the archived copy with
its original layout and styling. Two things matter about how it works:

- **Scripts never run in the viewer.** The archived page is a document, not
  the live site: its trackers and scripts are stripped out and the viewer
  runs it in a locked-down sandbox. What you see is exactly what was saved —
  nothing phones home, and nothing can execute on your machine.
- **It is a snapshot of a moment.** Pages that depend heavily on client-side
  scripting — feeds that load on scroll, embedded apps, maps — may not behave
  identically to the live site. PagePocket preserves the page as a document:
  the text, images, and layout you captured.

## Honest limits

- You can only save what your browser can see: a paywalled article is saved
  as far as you can open it, and content behind a login you have not signed
  into is not in the capture.
- Very large or resource-heavy pages can hit memory limits; the extension
  will tell you and clean up rather than fail silently. See
  [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).

## Deleting a saved page

Deleting a page from your library removes its archived copy and its search
index entry, and immediately disables any share links pointing at it. Local
ZIP exports are ordinary files — deleting them is up to you.
